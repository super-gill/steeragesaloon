# r8grow notes (round 8, 0.39.8, seed 42): bold but sane growth, 1900 to 1939, played straight through

No copies, no retries: every decision was taken once with the views, advice and forecasts in front of me, and lived with.

## Verdict
**Above both targets, by a wide margin.** NW £2,150.9k on 1 Jan 1914 (bold band £0.5m-£1m: 2.2 times the top) and £13,955.2k on 1 Jan 1939 (band £0.5m-£3m: 4.7 times the top). Other Januaries: 1905 £291.3k, 1910 £1,066.5k, 1920 £9,625.3k, 1921 £5,591.9k, 1929 £10,696.7k.
- **1900-1914: the start is slow for two years, then far too fast.** 1900 made £0.1k and the bank's cap (mortgage at most 0.7 x worth + headroom) held me to one ship until 1901 and refused Hesperia in Jan 1902. After that: 1902 profit £99k on three old second-hand ships, 1903 £146k, 1905 £212k. NW passed £0.5m in Oct 1906 (seven years before the target date) and £1m in Oct 1909. The 1907 panic cost about £270k of NW for a year (ship values x0.75, 9% of debt called) and the 1908 American slump was my best year yet (£230k). New 12,000-17,000-ton emigrant ships returned 22-27% a year on their cost in 1913 (Glen Affric £89k on £365k, Loch Shira £73k on £273k); old second-hand ones 28-41% (Hesperia £33k on £80k). I built about one ship a year from 1903 and borrowed at most about £600k; a less pushy owner would still have passed £1m before 1914.
- **1914-1921 (KI-077): the war roughly doubles NW on paper, the boom adds a third more, the crash and the duty take it back; in real terms about flat.** NW £2.15m (Jan 1914) → £2.98m (1915) → £3.78m (1916) → £4.89m (1917) → £5.72m (1918) → £6.04m (1919) → £9.63m (1920) → about £10.2m (Jul 1920) → £5.59m (Jan 1921) → £5.92m (1922). Why: trading was poor (1915 £144k, 1917 £137k; the Canadian and exp trades lost money, requisition hire was £2-5k a ship a month, no ship was lost in the whole war), but the fleet's book value rose with the war index (£2.00m Jan 1914 → £3.50m Jan 1916). In 1918 I sold two laid-up ships at 3 times their cost (EPD took 80% of the gain), and in 1919-20 sold four ships to syndicates for £3.79m (Loch Shira £681k, Glen Affric £1,426k, Loch Morven £573k, Ben Shira £1,111k; they had cost £1.16m). EPD took £219k (1918), £700k (1919), £955k (1920). In 1913 money (prices x0.42 → x1.05) the Line was worth about £2.2m in Jan 1921, about what it was worth in Jan 1914. That is defensible; the 1920s are not.
- **1921-1939: liner returns several times the period's, and the Depression bites only 1930-34.** Trading profit ran at about £0.57m-£0.95m a year 1922-29 on fleets worth £1.4m-£3.3m (25-40%); 1929 £1.23m. 1931-33 were about break-even in trading (the profit was Consols interest), which is better than round 7 and about right. But 1935-38 went straight back to 20-30% a year: Glen Coe (tourist liner, 1936, £1,225k) averaged £29-31k a month (about £360k a year) and 1937-38 made £1.06m-£1.08m each. NW in the 1930s was carried by idle money: £6.4m cash and £4.1m Consols of the £13.96m in Jan 1939 (Consols bought at 5.33% in Oct 1920 rose as yields fell to 2.9%).
- **Outside ships (0.39.8): they never came into my trades.** `S.otw[rk].k` stayed 0 on liv, exp, lha, hal, gny and waf all game. The trades are nowhere near the 85% trigger: in Jan 1939 the fare-weighted load (`outLoad`) was liv 39%, exp 22%, lha 39%. The returns above come from margins on ships that are well short of full, not from overfull trades, so the new mechanism does not touch this game's balance at all. A Norwegian line (Fjordheim, Dec 1918) and an Italian one (Stella Polare, Apr 1920) did start on liv as named new lines; both sold out within a few years.
- Imperial Atlantic did not fail in this seed (reconstructed Mar 1914, still trading in 1939); Nordmark failed Dec 1911 (I bought its Elbstern from the brokers at 70% of worth).

## Findings (most severe first)
- [balance, S1] (1902-1939) Ship returns far above the period's all game, and the 0.39.8 competition fix does not reach them. Numbers: 1913 new emigrant ships 22-27% a year on cost, old second-hand ones 28-41%; 1922-29 trading profit 25-40% a year on fleet worth; 1937 Glen Coe ~30% on her cost; Strath Affric (1913, cost £367k) £240k a year in 1926 at age 13. The outside ships only answer trades over 85% full, but my trades ran at 22-39% load (Jan 1939) while my ships still cleared these sums, so the excess is in fares versus costs (or my share of a thin market), not in fullness. Sure (`s.pl` sums, `S.annual`, `outLoad` by eval).
- [balance/exploit, S2] (Jun 1921, Jul 1931) The Combine's offer is a multiple of net worth including idle cash and Consols, even in the slump. Jun 1921: £9,143,000 for a Line worth £5.84m; Jul 1931, the trough: £15,767,000 for a Line worth £9.98m, of which about £7.6m was cash and Consols. js/trust.js: `amt = max(netWorth(), fleetValue()*0.5) * (1.3 + R*0.3)`. Accepting ends the game, but it is a way to turn £1 of cash into £1.3-1.6 that no buyer would pay. Suggest offering on the fleet and goodwill plus the cash at par. Sure (code and two offers seen).
- [bug, tool] (1 Jan 1925; round 7 saw the same on 1 Jan 1936) `step` with `["propose","build",true]` plays no months, uses up the proposal and places no order. Steps: Traffic Department proposal "Build a passenger-cargo liner for Liverpool ↔ Halifax"; `step r8grow '[[...tourist refits...],["propose","build",true],["build","tourist",{...}]]' 6`. Result: the report still says 1 January 1925 (the PLAYED line repeats the previous step's), `S.props` is empty, `S.orders` has only the hand-built order. Cause: `propyes` for a build proposal runs its act `build`, which calls `openDesigner()` (js/advice.js:506-507); the headless month loop then does not advance. Afterwards I built her by hand. Sure.
- [bug?, yard] (Apr 1905, Apr 1911; maybe Jan 1925) An order at a yard quoted "slip free <this month>" sat "waiting" for a year while rivals took every slip there and other yards had free ones. Apr 1911: Strath Affric ordered at Elbe (quote "slip free April 1911", m=135), drawings approved m=137, slips at m=138 `us:126, rival:155, rival:149`; Solent had two free. Apr 1905 the same at Mersey (both slips rival until Oct 1906 / Aug 1907). The 0.35.6 rule keeps a free slip for each order in the drawing office or waiting (`free.length > held`, js/yard.js:233), so a rival should not have taken the last one. Each time I cancelled (lost the 10% paid: £26.5k and £35.5k) and reordered elsewhere. About 70% sure it is a bug rather than slips that were already gone when quoted; the quote is misleading in either case.
- [odd, departments] (1924-1934) The Traffic Department ignores mail contracts. It moved the passenger ships off exp in Dec 1924 and the exp mail was cancelled 1 Mar 1925 "after missed sailings"; it laid up Glen Etive, the only passenger ship on liv, in Dec 1931 and Jan 1934, each followed by "No mail was landed on Liverpool → New York last month". A department that moves or lays up the last mail ship should warn, or count the mail in its sums. Fairly sure (news lines "Traffic Department: Move ..."; mail warnings).
- [odd] (1 Dec 1920 to Jan 1921) A rate war declared by a line that ceased to exist the same day: "Scheldemond Line has sold its last two ships and been wound up" and "Dominion Pacific Line and Scheldemond Line fall out on Glasgow ↔ Halifax" are both dated 1 Dec 1920; `S.wars.hal.by` = ["dominion","schelde"] in Jan 1921 ("RATE WAR" on hal although I was in the conference). Sure.
- [odd] (Jul 1924, Jul 1933) Traffic Department "Put idle cash to work" proposals for ships no longer on the market (Balnevis II, Lochness) stay under Needs attention; accepting is "refused". Sure.
- [odd] (Apr 1913) Two ships named Glen Affric: mine (ordered Jul 1910) and Caledon & Clyde's 1905 Glen Affric, sold to Navigazione Partenope and moved to ham in Apr 1913 under the same name. `nameTaken` (js/helpers.js:171) is used for rival and market names but not checked for the player's own orders.
