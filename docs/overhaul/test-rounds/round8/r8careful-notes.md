# r8careful notes (round 8, 0.39.8, seed 41)

Player: a sensible, cautious owner of the period, played straight through (no copies, no retries). 1 Jan 1900 to 1 Jan 1939.

## Verdict
**Above both targets, by about 2x in 1914 and 2.4x in 1939.** The game is finished: the slot stands at 1 January 1939.
- January 1914: **£533,468**, against a careful band of £100k to £250k (2.1x the top). I was inside the band from January 1907 (£140,369) to January 1910 (£190,788); the top was passed in October 1910 (£248,002) and January 1911 (£254,735), with no debt and only second-hand ships bought.
- January 1939: **£7,256,213**, against £0.5m to £3m for any owner (2.4x the top), with no takeovers, no shares, no borrowing after 1921 and never more than five ships. The £3m top was passed in July 1926 (£3.11m). In 1939 the Line is £5.51m Consols, £0.74m cash and five ships worth about £0.95m.
- Other Januaries: 1920 £2,577,588; 1929 £3,900,497.

Why (most important first):
1. **Ships pay 40% to 85% of what they cost, every normal year, 1910-1913 and 1922-1938.** Hesperia (1890, paid £78.6k) made £46,143 in 1912 (106% of her worth); Glenmorven (new 1915, £263k) £180,001 in 1923; Stella Maris (bought 1921 £206k) £169,575 in 1923, £135,191 in 1937 and £148,367 in 1938; **Ardmorven (built 1911 for £244k) still made £139,522 in 1938 at 27 years old**; Sabine (cargo, bought 1931 £63k) £55,929 in 1932, the trough. A careful owner needs no skill to compound that: 1922-1929 averaged £430k a year from three or four ships.
2. **Outside ships never touched my trades.** They key on a trade's load (85%), but my trades ran at 0.4 to 0.77 loaded (liv 0.52-0.72, lha 0.38-0.77) while each ship still cleared £100k+ a year: the money is in the margin per passenger, not in full ships. The only trade where they came (cot, after Mersey & Gulf failed in 1931) stalled at k 0.27 for three years at load 0.93, and the cargo ship there still made 74-88% of her price a year until a named new line arrived in Nov 1934.
3. **The war and the boom double the Line on paper** (£533k 1914 → £2.58m 1920) through ship worths (Glenmorven £1.04m in Jan 1920 against £263k cost), then the 1920-21 crash takes most of it back (£1.14m in Jan 1921). War profits themselves were modest (1915 a loss of £15,726; 1916-18 £75k-£102k).
4. **Consols did the rest after 1929.** I parked £2.4m of retained 1920s profit in Consols at 4.5-5.6% before the Crash; they paid about £120-170k a year and their price rose about £1.2m by 1934. The Depression itself bit properly on the ships (trading near nil in 1931, Line profit £122,744 against £480,991 in 1929; two ships laid up 1931-32), so 1929-35 is survivable and not trivial for the ships, but trivially survivable for the Line because of its 1920s cash.

Note on method: I used the advice heavily (moves, refits, lay-ups) and set fares to the line rate each quarter. I declined every Combine offer (the last, £11,198,000 in Nov 1938, is 1.5x my net worth).
