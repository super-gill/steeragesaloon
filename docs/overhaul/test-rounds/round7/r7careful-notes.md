# r7careful notes (round 7, 0.39.6, seed 31)

Player: a sensible, cautious owner of the period. 1 Jan 1900 to 1 Jan 1939. Game finished: the slot stands at 1 January 1939.

## Result against the targets
**Above both targets.**
- January 1914: **£476,085**, against a careful band of £100k-£250k (1.9x the top). The top of the band was passed in January 1911 (£258,630); I was inside the band from January 1907 (£163,062) to January 1910 (£220,764).
- January 1939: **£5,764,730**, against £0.5m-£3m for any owner (1.9x the top), with no takeovers, no shares, no borrowing after 1923, and never more than five ships. The £3m top was passed in January 1926 (£3,057,905). In 1939 the Line is £3.43m cash, £1.40m Consols and four ships worth £0.87m (built 1912-1920).
- Other Januaries: 1920 £1,988,526; 1929 £3,719,802.

Why:
1. **1900-1913: old second-hand ships still pay 40-75% of their worth a year.** Morven (1881) £22,727 on a worth of £30,355 in 1913; Hesperia (1890, paid £78,000) about £34k in 1905 and £30,923 in 1913. The dearer 0.39.6 prices made new building unattractive (a new emigrant ship forecast about 10% in 1909; Glenmorven, built 1912 for £245,000, returned 24% in 1913), so a careful owner buys old ships, and those still pay. Still, I was inside the careful band until 1910; 0.39.6 brought 1900-1910 about right.
2. **1914: Imperial Atlantic failed on 1 Feb 1914 and left Liverpool → New York nearly empty.** My cash rose £135k in Jan-Jul 1914 (more than all of 1913) and 1914 profit was £243k. This, not my moves, is most of the gap at January 1914 (£476k then £594k by July).
3. **The war and the boom double the Line on paper.** £476k (1914) → £1.99m (1920). Profits in the war were modest (£63k-£125k a year after EPD); the rest is ship values marked up (fleet £0.95m in Jan 1918 against about £0.4m pre-war) and the sale of three old ships into the 1918-20 market for £314k (Morven 1881 £95k, Hesperia 1890 £97k, Caledonian 1886 £121k) and Strathmore (1903) £285k. I did not take the £1.38m syndicate offer for Glenmorven (it lapsed in my 3-month step).
4. **1922-1938: four ships make £220k-£414k a year on routes the rivals left empty.** Ships bought cheap in the 1921-23 glut (Aurora Borealis £244k, Strathmore £180k, Loch Etive II £141k) and Glenmorven (1912) each made 36-79% of what was paid, every year, and the Depression's worst year for me was 1931 at £121k profit. With nothing to spend it on the cash piled up: £2.5m of the 1939 net worth is retained profit after tax.
