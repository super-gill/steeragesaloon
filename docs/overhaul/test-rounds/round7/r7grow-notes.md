# r7grow notes (round 7, 0.39.6, seed 32): bold but sane growth, 1900 to 1939

## Verdict
**Above both targets.** Net worth £1,337.7k on 1 Jan 1914 (bold band £0.5m-£1m: about a third over the top) and £17,687.9k on 1 Jan 1939 (band £0.5m-£3m: about six times the top). Other Januaries: 1905 £99.8k, 1910 £546.7k, 1920 £8,164.1k, 1921 £5,350.8k, 1929 £12,221.4k.
- **1900-1914: close to the band, but over.** 0.39.6 made the start slow and that part works: £2.0k profit in 1900, NW under £100k until 1905, and the bank's limit (it counts the 60% mortgage) kept me from buying a second ship until 1901. The jump comes in 1905 (profit £50k → £171k) and from the new 11,000-13,000-ton emigrant ships ordered from 1905: each returned 20-30% a year on her cost in 1910-13 (Ben Shira, £332k, about £96k in 1913; Loch Shira, £276k, about £72k), and the old second-hand Silver Wave (£106k in 1902) about 45%. The 1907 panic hurt (bank failure, a called loan, and I had to sell four old ships to save Loch Morven's delivery) but cost only about a year. I crossed £0.5m in Oct 1909 and £1m in Jun 1912. What pushed 1914 over: profits of £316k (1911), £330k (1912), £419k (1913), the failure of Imperial Atlantic and Nordmark on 1 Dec 1913 (December 1913 netted £69.9k, the best month so far, in the dead season), and borrowing to keep 2 ships on order at a time. A slightly less pushy owner would land at about £1m.
- **1914-1921 (KI-077): the war and the boom pay well, after the EPD not absurdly.** NW £1.71m (Jul 1914) → £2.54m (Jan 1915) → £3.62m (1916) → £4.39m (1917) → £4.96m (1918) → £5.28m (1919) → £8.16m (Jan 1920) → about £9.3m (Aug-Sep 1920) → £5.35m (Jan 1921). Why: ship values rose with the war (fleet worth £3.04m in Jan 1915 at ship index 0.54, £3.78m in Jan 1916 at 0.73), the requisitioned ships earned a steady fixed hire, five sunk troopships were paid their agreed value (£1.29m in all, about what they cost), two old tramps bought for £108.6k together earned £80k-100k a year each in 1915-16, and in 1919-20 syndicates paid £1,158,000 for Glen Shira (contract £280k) and £1,728,000 for Ben Shira (£332k). The EPD caught much of the sales (£607k on 1919, £1,385k on 1920). In 1913 money (prices x0.42 → x1.05) the Line was worth about £2.1m in Jan 1921, 1.6 times its Jan 1914 figure, and about £3.3m at the 1920 peak. That is generous but defensible for the period's great war fortunes; the 1920s are not.
- **1921-1939: the 1920s and the Depression are far too profitable.** With ships at half price in 1921-23 against fares still at the post-war level, I bought five receivers' and brokers' ships and four more in 1923; from then on trading profit ran at about 50% a year on the fleet's worth (1923: about £1.14m on £2.31m) with profits of £0.95m-£1.34m every year 1922-1928. Palisades (bought for £277.9k in 1921) made £18k-23k a month for five years; Valentia (bought for £157.0k from Imperial's receivers in 1914) £17k-22k a month (about 150% a year). The Depression bit only in 1930 (£351k) and 1931 (£62k, with £150k of Consols interest in it, so a trading loss of about £100k); 1932 was back to £655k with nine ships (trading about £0.5m on a fleet worth £1.26m), 1935-38 £0.93m-1.27m, even through a combine and conference expulsion in 1936-38. By 1939 £10.9m sat idle in cash and £3.9m in Consols. Real lines earned 5-10% on capital in the 1920s and lost money in 1931-33.
- **Imperial Atlantic in the 1909-12 boom:** it was reconstructed by its bankers in Feb 1911 (`S.rivals.imperial.rescued` = 133), then built two 45,000-ton giants (Atlantean Jun 1911, Hyperborean Mar 1912, lost 5 Apr 1912 with 1,599 dead), and failed for good on 1 Dec 1913 (29 ships, 2 to the brokers, 27 for scrap or abroad), the same day as Nordmark (rescued Jan 1910). Its trades (lha, hal, liv, stl, gny, exp, ham) opened up just before the war. What it did to me: December 1913 was my best month of the decade, I bought its Valentia at 70% of worth, and the forecast for liv rose sharply.

## Strategy and state left
Buy cheap old emigrant ships 1901-1906 and put them where head office forecasts best (liv, lha, hal); lavish tables, agents both sides, piers at Halifax, Liverpool, Glasgow and New York, bunker contracts, mails; from 1905 order a new 11,000-13,000-ton emigrant ship whenever cash allows; join the conference in 1908. Repay all debt and go into Consols in the war; sell to the syndicates in 1919-20; buy cheap in 1921-23; four departments acting from 1931. Game finished at 1 Jan 1939: 11 ships, £10.9m cash, £3.9m Consols, no debt; combine against the Line until Dec 1938, out of the conference since Dec 1936.
