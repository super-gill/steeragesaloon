## Against the round 6 targets
- January 1914: £595,362, against a careful band of £100k-£250k: 2.4x the top, inside the bold band (£0.5m-£1m). The careful band's top was passed in Jan 1909 (£253,113).
- January 1939: £7,759,489, against £0.5m-£3m for any owner: 2.6x the top, with no takeovers, no borrowing after 1913 and £1.5m-£2m idling in Consols from 1925. The 1939 band's top was passed in Jan 1927 (£3.38m).
- Why: (1) 1902-1908, before the conference forms, old second-hand emigrant ships return 70%-120% of their worth a year, so £26k becomes £137k by 1906 on three old ships and no new building; (2) a new emigrant ship under the conference still returns about 32% (Glenmorven 1910); (3) the war adds about 26% in real terms, almost all as ship values, and the 1919-20 boom lets a careful owner sell three ships for £1.07m; (4) 1921-1938, cargo and intermediate ships bought in the 1921-23 slump return 46%-200% a year, Glenshira (1912) makes 70%-130% of her worth a year into the 1930s, and the Depression's steerage trough is 30% of 1929, not a tenth, so the worst year (1931) still made £136k; (5) the 1937 bank failure no longer happens. None of my moves was unusual for a cautious owner: buy one ship at a time, repay debt, sell old tonnage into the boom, keep a third of the Line in cash and Consols.
