## Findings
- [bug/odd] (7 Mar 1904) A solvent Line is foreclosed the same day a one-off inquiry bill lands, with no chance to sell a ship. Repro: slot q2rep (seed 22) with the actions in this log; Jan 1904 Silver Wave lost; 7 Mar 1904 inquiry £45.2k; cash -£15.7k < -odLimit £9,956; bankRealise has nothing to sell (no stock), rescueTry rolls (chance about 0.72-0.2 with cover < 0.8) and fails; S.over='bust'. NW after the bill was +£17.8k and the fleet (Morven, Caledonian, Tay Castle) was worth about £100k against £65k debt. The advice the same moment says "Raise cash: sell SS Tay Castle ... about £32,820". A real bank would give a solvent customer time to sell a ship (or the claims would be paid over months); the foreclosure check (js/sim.js:358) fires on the day. Suggest: a lump legal bill is payable over a few months, or bankRealise sells a ship first when NW > 0. Sure (reproduced).
- [odd] (Mar 1904) The court blames "she had no wireless, and help came late" for a 1904 loss. In 1904 only a few express liners carried Marconi sets (the game's own news, Feb 1900: "It is dear, and only the great lines can afford it for now"); no court would fault a 7,500-ton emigrant ship for lacking one until about 1909-12 (Republic 1909, the 1912 rules). blameOf (js/economy.js:322) adds 1 for no wireless at any date. Gate it to about 1910 on the 1900 calendar. Sure (code).
- [odd, low] (Oct 1903) A second-hand ship comes with her old master, here a drinker (Silver Wave, Capt Robert Munro, veteran,drinker), and head office did not advise replacing him in Jan 1904 although it advises replacing a merely "lax" master (Jan 1900). Pool had a cautious master free. Sure (eval of S.market captains).

- [odd] (Nov 1904, q2bold2) A second total loss within a year: SS Hesperia (1890, 6,800grt, cond 78%, bought Jul 1904) posted missing 24 Nov 1904 at 47N 48W, 420 dead ("taking water", grave). Together with Silver Wave in the q2bold timeline that is two of five ships, 924 dead, in 1904. The inquiry (3 Jan 1905, blame 1.5) found "no wireless" and "she was being driven at full speed": full speed was head office's own advice of Oct 1904 ("Run SS Hesperia at full speed ... the extra crossings ... outweigh the extra coal"), which says nothing of the risk or the inquiry. Bill £34.7k on a Line worth about £120k; I had to sell Caledonian the day before to stay above the foreclosure line. British North Atlantic emigrant lines lost very few ships at sea in 1900-13; a small line losing two in a year is far outside the period. Sure about the events; the rate is two samples.
- [harness] (Apr 1905) press("shoresell",...) prints "done" but does nothing: shoresell is not in DOING (js/advice.js:443), so press returns the string 'not an action button', which the report shows as done. The direct action shoresell("pier:HAL",1) works. Sure.
- [bug] (Oct 1905, q2bold2) A ship lost at sea comes back on the second-hand market: "Hesperia" built 1890, 6,800grt, 14kn, at Naples, £69,400 (market id 95), eleven months after SS Hesperia (id 76) was posted missing with 420 dead (S.gone holds {id:76,name:'Hesperia',kind:'lost'}). refreshMarket (js/sim.js ~line 436) draws from TEMPL filtered by nameTaken (js/helpers.js:170), which checks only ships afloat, rivals, the market and orders, not S.gone. A sold ship (Caledonian) coming back is fine; a sunk one is not. Sure (eval).
- [bug, S2] (Aug 1906 to Apr 1907, q2bold2) An order whose keel payment could not be paid is billed the keel again every time it pays its arrears. Loch Morven (Woolston, £179,000, ordered Jul 1906) log: m80 "Could not pay £35,800 at the keel laying"; m81 "Arrears of £35,800 paid. Work resumes." then the same month "Could not pay £35,800 at the keel laying"; m86 the same again. Paid £89,500 (order £17.9k + the keel twice) and still owes a third £35,800, with the ship never laid down. Cause (js/yard.js ~277): in 'waiting', bill(o,0.2) fails, adds the keel to o.due and frees the slip, leaving the order 'waiting'; ordersMonth pays o.due as arrears and then, still 'waiting', bills the keel afresh. The arrears payment should lay the keel (or bill() should not add to due when it gives up the slip). A Line short of cash loses 20% of the price each round. Sure (eval of S.orders[].log).
- [bug, same cause] (Jan-Mar 1912) The launch bill loops the same way: Ben Shira (£200k, Elbe) could not pay £60,000 at the launch on 1 Jan 1912 (the day income tax was taken); on 1 Mar 1912 the arrears were paid and the launch billed again at once, so she has £180,000 paid at 69% built (order £20k + keel £40k + launch twice). bill(o,0.3) in the plating branch (js/yard.js ~292) re-bills because prog stays at 0.62 and the stage stays 'plating'. The extra £60k should come back as a negative delivery bill. Sure (eval of S.orders).
- [odd] (1 Mar 1912; also 1 Oct 1906) "Raise cash: sell SS Glen Affric ... has earned least for what she is worth over the last year" for a ship handed over that morning with no voyages (Oct 1906 it named Glen Morven, four months old, in a rate war). The pick ranks by last year's earnings over worth and so always names the newest ship, which is the one the Line can least afford to sell. Sure.
- [odd] (1 Oct 1912) As in r6, Imperial Atlantic, the largest rival and a Combine member, fails in the boom: "Of its 29 ships, 2 are offered through the brokers, 27 go for scrap or abroad." The tonnage leaves the North Atlantic entirely, so by Jan 1913 Liverpool → New York has 4 rival ships (5,293 third berths) and Liverpool ↔ Halifax 3 rival ships against my 7. Head office forecasts jump: every one of my 12,000-ton emigrant ships about £11k a month on liv against £5-6k on lha (Stralsund II, bought for £102k, £12.4k a month = 146% a year). After I moved four ships off lha (copy check), each of the eight forecast £6.8k-£10.9k. In 1912-13 the real trades were full and Cunard/White Star/CPR were adding ships; a failed line's ships would be bought by others and stay in the trade. Sure (eval of S.rships by route).
- [odd, balance] (1913) Ship-level returns over the 12 months to Jan 1914 (ship ledger, before office, interest and tax): Stralsund II £106k on £102k paid (104%), Lüneburg II £76k on £81k (93%), Ben Shira £134k on £200k (67%), Glen Affric £136k on £222k (61%), Loch Affric £120k on £216k (56%), Ben Morven £111k on £214k (52%), Glen Morven £94k on £192k (49%), Loch Shira £103k on £216k (48%). Before Imperial Atlantic failed (1911-12) the same new ships made £4-7k a month (25-40%). 1913 taxable profit £899k on a fleet worth £1.44m.
- [balance] (1912-13) Load factor and the steerage pool on lha/hal, from the sailing cables sampled monthly (S.wire) and from legCalc with and without the pool (routeCapRaw stubbed in a read-only eval): Jul-Dec 1912 outbound 50-71% of all berths (third 52-75%), homeward 14-21%; Jan-Dec 1913 outbound 77-80% (third exactly 80%, the conference cap Math.floor(b*0.8)), homeward 26-58%. Round trip about 40% in late 1912, 50-60% in 1913. The pool never bound: legCalc gave identical steerage with and without it on every lha ship (Jun 1912, Jul 1912, May 1913, Jan 1914) and under 0.5% less on Glen Affric (hal). What held the ships back in 1913 was the 80% third-class cap: without the conference they would have carried 1,827-2,084 outbound instead of 1,663-1,667 (Jan 1914). The pool caps a member at its share of berths sailing x1.15, which cannot bind when the Line has most of the berths on the trade (7 of 10 ships on lha after Imperial's failure). Sure (evals).
