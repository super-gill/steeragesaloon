### Main balance finding
- [odd, S2] (1900-1914) 0.39.5 slows the start but not the boom. NW by January: 1901 £33.3k, 1902 £30.5k, 1903 £50.9k, 1904 £128.7k, 1905 £156.4k, 1906 £267.5k, 1907 £392.2k, 1908 £419.6k, 1909 £834.3k, 1910 £1,378.1k, 1911 £2,016.0k, 1912 £2,550.8k, 1913 £3,376.1k, 1914 £4,351.3k: 4.3 times the top of the bold band (£1m), and above r6's £3.81m. Up to 1908 I ran at about half of r6 (forecasts for second-hand ships were 1-4k a month, not 7k, and rivals answered every ship I added with one of theirs), but year profits from 1909 were far higher than r6's: 1909 £746k (r6 £445k), 1910 £833k (£566k), 1912 £1,079k (£743k), 1913 £1,160k (£858k). Once the conference opened (Jan 1908) and lending came back (Oct 1908), 13,000-ton emigrant ships forecast at 7-9k a month on about £240k (35-45% a year) and delivered about that. 1908, the slump year, was again higher than 1907 (£370k against £218k). Real liners: 5-10% (round 6), or 12-18% in good years (0.39.5's own comment). Sure about the numbers (finance view and per-ship ledgers).
