Trading during the war was modest: 1915 £255k, 1916 £505k, 1917 £266k, 1918 £506k (requisition hire about £2-4k a ship a month; 15 of 19 ships requisitioned by 1917). EPD took nothing in 1914-19 (my standard was the 1911-13 average, £738k) and £1.70m on 1920. 1914 to 1919 the Line went 2.4x in money but only 1.13x in real terms; the real gain came in 1919-20 from selling ships into the boom, which the game announces ("Ship prices are at their height", Apr 1920). Verdict: the war years themselves do not pay too well for a big liner company in this build; the 1919-20 boom does, because its top is named and syndicates bid 5-7x pre-war worth every month or two (I sold 7 ships in Jul 1919 to Jul 1920 for £4.22m that had cost the Line about £1.09m). A smaller line (the keen script) gains more relative to its size because requisition hire and inflated ship values are large against a small base and EPD's 6%-of-net-worth standard is low.
