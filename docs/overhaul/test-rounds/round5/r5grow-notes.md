- [exploit] (Apr 1910) A failed rival's nearly new express liner is sold far below what the same ship costs to build, and earns far more than a player-built one. Nordmark Line failed 1 Jan 1910; on 1 Apr 1910 the brokers offered SS Nordstern (1907, 24,500grt, 23.5kn) at £147,600, against her worth of £211,552. `look r5grow build express '{"grt":24500,"knots":23.5,"line":"exp"}'` prices the same ship new at £754,000 (forecast -3k/mo on exp); Nordstern, bought with `["buy","Nordstern","exp"]` (£59k down), cleared £6.6k, £21.8k and £13.0k in her first three months and averages £13-14k/mo (about £165k a year, more than her price). Why: market ships carry `base` in 1921 pounds (637,000 x prices 0.42 = £267k as new), while a design is priced in today's money at 2.8 times that; she has 600 first-class berths against 330 for the same tonnage designed by the player; and her coal uses the generic `fastK` 1.87 against a design's fuelK 2.27, with crewK 1 against 1.39. A real owner could not buy a three-year-old Kaiser-class express for a fifth of her replacement cost and get it back in a year. Sure (bought her, numbers from `look eval` on S.ships and designStats).

## KI-065 answer
- A human playing boldly but sanely passed the scripted advisor's £950k (Jan 1914) by October 1910 and stood at £3.53M on 1 January 1914, about 3.7 times the advisor and 39 times the cautious script.
- Drivers, in order: (1) the conference: joining in Oct 1908 removed rate-war risk, so I could put 9 or 10 ships on lha and charge first class 50% over the line rate; the advisor can never join (its buttons are `tabgo`). (2) New 11,000grt emigrant ships delivered 1909-1913 that cleared £9k-£13k a month each in 1912-13 on a cost of £176k-£196k, i.e. 60-80% a year, about double the 37-40% the research note measured for the advisor's. (3) Nordstern, a 24,500grt express bought for £147.6k in a receiver's sale, clearing about £165k a year. (4) Compounding: profits of £415k (1910), £475k (1911), £1.02M (1912), £1.33M (1913) all went back into ships.
- Plausibility: not plausible for the era. Real Atlantic emigrant lines earned perhaps 5-15% on capital in the boom (Cunard's best pre-war years paid 10-20% dividends on a much larger capital). A one-ship 1900 start (£25k) becoming a £3.5M line in 14 years, and £25.7M by 1940 with no year of real distress after 1908, is far too easy. Reducing KI-065 needs: a fare ceiling or tension inside the conference, lower boom-year steerage loads/returns for new tonnage, and pricing receiver's-sale express liners nearer their replacement cost.
