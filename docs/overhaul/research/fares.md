# Passenger Fares and Volumes by Class, 1900 to 1940

Research notes for a British shipping-line sim. Figures are as found in the sources listed at the end. "~" marks approximations or my rounding. "(summ.)" marks figures read through a page summariser rather than a verbatim table; treat as good but check the original before relying on the exact digit. Fares are one-way per adult unless stated.

Currency reference (not from the sources opened; standard gold parity): GBP 1 = about USD 4.86 before 1914 and 1925 to 1931. One source (Encyclopedia Titanica thread) gives "$4.86 to the pound" for 1912.

## 1. Transatlantic: UK (Liverpool/Southampton) to New York

US head tax rose from 50 cents (1882) to $4 by 1907 (Hatton ANU paper).

### 1b. Cunard averages by class, Liverpool to New York (Keeling, EBHA 2008, GBP) (summ.)

| Class | 1885 to 1899 avg fare | 1900 to 1914 avg fare | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steerage westbound | 3.5 | 5.1 | +44% |
| Steerage eastbound | 3.6 | 5.4 | +49% |
| Second cabin | 7.5 | 9.8 | +31% |
| First cabin | 18.3 | 26.0 | +42% |

- Normal steerage rate ~GBP 4.8 to 5.0; in fare-war periods ~GBP 2.5 to 2.6 (roughly half).
- Seasonal steerage difference only 3 to 5% (winter GBP 4.1 vs summer 4.2 westbound, 1885 to 1914).
- Cunard revenue per passenger (all classes) ~$100 1895 to 1908, ~$150 after 1908 (Streamliner Memories, citing cartel effect).

### 1c. First cabin (Dupont, Keeling, Weiss, NBER w22426, USD, New York to UK)

| Year | Advertised first-class fare |
|---|---|
| 1889 | $55 (low point) |
| 1900 | $84 |
| 1908 | $124 conference minimum for Lusitania/Mauretania |
| 1914 | $99 |

Cunard actual average first-class revenue 1883 to 1914 ~$120 to $150, i.e. 30 to 50% above advertised minimums (people buy better cabins).

### 1f. Wartime 1914 to 1918

Gap. No source opened gave wartime fares. Only data point: 1919 Naples/Trieste to NY steerage $75, first $250 (see 2c), which shows post-war levels about double pre-war.

### 4d. Interwar North Atlantic totals by class

Gap. No Conference annual figures by class for 1919 to 1939 were found in an openable source. Fragments: first half 1931 carried 127,000 fewer passengers than first half 1930 (Wikipedia, Nieuw Amsterdam); Britannic's best 1933 crossing was 1,003 passengers, under 65% of capacity; Minnekahda carried 6,228 tourists in 10 round trips in 1925; Tourist Third Cabin numbers rose every year 1926 to 1929 on Olympic (Chirnside, forum). The shape is clear from immigration law: quotas from 1921/1924 cut third class hard, tourist traffic of Americans grew to 1929, then the Depression roughly halved traffic in the early 1930s.

## 5. Onboard spending

Thin. Found only: deck chair rental about $1 per voyage (1910 guide); Prohibition made US-flag ships dry, and Leviathan's inability to sell liquor is cited as one reason she never made money; booze cruise economics relied on bar takings over fares. Pre-1914 fares included meals but not wine, beer or spirits (NDL 1888). No per-passenger bar revenue figure found.

## Reliability note

- Strongest: Hatton annual steerage index (1900 to 1913), Keeling Conference volume table (1898 to 1914), Keeling Cunard class averages, Dupont/Keeling/Weiss first-class series, Board of Trade outward totals, Spectator 1937 cruise adverts (primary), Wikipedia ship articles quoting 1930s HAL fares (which cite contemporary NYT).
- Medium: GG Archives 1904 press compilation (primary newspaper excerpts but summarised), actual ticket prices (single data points, may include rail or several people), Queen Mary 1936 fares (secondary blog).
- Weak or gaps: wartime 1914 to 1918 fares; 1920s first/second/third conference minimums by year; interwar class volumes; River Plate and interwar Australia fares; onboard spend.
- All pages were read via a summarising fetch tool; tables marked (summ.) were not re-checked verbatim.
