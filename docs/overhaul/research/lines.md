# Real lines, ships and technology dates, 1900-1940

Research notes for a 1900-1940 shipping-line sim. All facts below come from pages actually opened (see Sources). "~" = approximate. "UNVERIFIED" = believed but not confirmed by an opened source. "GAP" = searched, not found.

## Reliability note

- Most figures come from Wikipedia ship and company pages, read via a summarising fetch tool. Ship GRTs, dates and Blue Riband speeds are consistent across pages and are solid.
- Company fleet sizes for exactly 1900 were largely NOT found. Where a figure exists it is for another year (NDL 1890 and 1914, HAPAG 1892 and 1914, INC 1902). Treat fleet sizes as the weakest section.
- Two sources disagree on the Cunard 1903 loan (GBP 2.6m vs 2.5m) and on the White Star sale to Royal Mail (1926 GBP 7m vs 1927 GBP 7.9m). Both versions are recorded.
- GRT is a volume measure (100 cu ft per ton), not weight. Different national rules gave different GRTs for the same ship (Leviathan, see section 7).

## 2. IMM and the Cunard 1903 deal

**IMM (International Mercantile Marine Co.)**
- Announced 1 Oct 1902 by J.P. Morgan & Co. Founders/partners: Clement Griscom (American/Red Star, via INC), Bernard N. Baker (Atlantic Transport), J. Bruce Ismay (White Star), John Ellerman (Leyland).
- Lines: American Line, Red Star, White Star (1902-1927), Leyland (1902-1927), Atlantic Transport (1901-1931), Dominion (1902-1926), 51% of Holland America (1902-1917). Cunard stayed out.
- Overleveraged; defaulted on bond interest late 1914; receivership 1915; emerged 1916.
- White Star sold to Royal Mail group: IMM page says 1926 for GBP 7m; White Star page says 1927 for GBP 7.9m (agreement late 1926, completion 1927 is the likely reconciliation, UNVERIFIED).
- Fleet 30 ships (1930), 19 (1933), 11 (1935). 1931 merged with Roosevelt Steamship Co, acquiring United States Lines; renamed United States Lines Inc. 1943.

**Cunard-government agreement 1903**
- Loan GBP 2.6m over 20 years at 2.75% (Lusitania page) or GBP 2.5m (Cunard page).
- Annual subsidy GBP 75,000 per ship (i.e. GBP 150,000 for two), plus mail contract GBP 68,000.
- Conditions: built to Admiralty specification for use as armed merchant cruisers; minimum 24 kn service speed; (Cunard to stay British, widely reported, UNVERIFIED here).

**Later subsidy: Queen Mary.** Work halted Dec 1931, resumed 1934. Government loan conditional on Cunard-White Star merger (10 May 1934, Cunard ~62%, White Star creditors ~38%); loan covered completing Queen Mary and building a running mate. Exact amount (often quoted GBP 9.5m under the North Atlantic Shipping Act 1934): UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Conferences and rate wars

| Item | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin-fare agreements UK-US lines | 1896, 1898 | Start of "mostly sustained collusion" on cabin pricing (NBER w22426) |
| Continental steerage pools (New York Continental Conference; German-led pool, NDLV) | Early 1890s | Pool agreements confirmed for early 1890s (Feys). Exact 1892 NDLV founding date: UNVERIFIED |
| 1904 steerage rate war | May to Nov 1904 | Started by HAPAG in May to break Cunard's Fiume (Austro-Hungarian) emigrant contract. Lines: HAPAG, NDL, White Star, Cunard, Dominion, Allan, Anchor, CP. Fares fell from USD 20-30 to USD 7-10 (GBP 2 from British ports; Keeling: ~GBP 2.5 vs normal ~GBP 5). Ended late Nov with German lines conceding; rates back to USD 35-37; spheres agreed |
| Atlantic Conference | 5 Feb 1908 | 13 lines incl. Cunard, White Star, HAPAG, Holland America, CP. Fined members; drove out Russian Volunteer Fleet; forced Russian American Line in (Sept 1909) |
| US antitrust suit | 4 Jan 1911 | Sherman Act suit against the 13; contracts expiring, litigation discouraged renewal |

Typical steerage fares 1900-1914 (Keeling, sterling averages): westbound GBP 5.1, eastbound GBP 5.4 (vs GBP 3.5 / 3.6 in 1885-99). Passenger numbers fell during the 1904 cheap-fare period.

German return to New York:
- HAPAG: contract with Harriman group 1920; transatlantic service resumed 1921 jointly with United American Lines (Hamburg-Southampton-Cherbourg-New York); HAPAG bought UAL 1926. Albert Ballin (1923) in fleet.
- NDL: New York service again from 1921-22 (agency arrangement with United States Lines).
- Bremen: maiden 16 Jul 1929, 51,656 GRT, took Blue Riband on maiden voyage. Europa: 49,746 GRT, entered service 1930, took westbound record 1930. HAPAG-NDL cooperation 1930, joint Atlantic ops 1935.
