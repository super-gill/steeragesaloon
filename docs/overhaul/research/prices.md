## 6. Spending power context (who could afford first/second class)

UK:
- c.1909-13 clerks (BA Economics Section enquiry, via Scott and Walker 2014): railway and commercial clerks about £80/yr; bank clerks £90; civil service clerks £95; teachers £89-£130. Average adult railway clerk under £85 (1911) rising to about £90 (1913).
- Income tax exemption limit £160/yr (1910). Contemporary convention: income-tax-paying households (over £160) were the middle class; this was a small minority before 1914.
- Average earner (BoE series): about £47/yr in 1900, £54/yr in 1913, £107/yr in 1925, £102/yr in 1935 (weekly figure x 52, my arithmetic).
- GAP: Routh (Occupation and Pay in Great Britain 1906-60) has the standard occupational class averages for 1913/14, 1922/24, 1935/36 but I could not open a copy online.

US:
- 1900 occupational annual pay (Workers of the Nation, via Univ. Missouri): lawyers $1,200, surgeons $1,625, bankers/brokers $1,600, electricians $1,550, journalists $950, waiters $500, servants $144 plus board. All-industry average $438.
- 1920s: full-time non-farm employee about $1,350-1,500/yr (BLS Bull. 852).
- 1935-36: the National Resources Committee "Consumer Incomes in the US" is the standard source for family income distribution, but the PDF was too large to open (GAP; commonly quoted median is about $1,160 but I did not verify it).
- 1939: median full-year white male wage/salary $1,419 (Census).

## Notes on reliability

- All pre-1914 UK and US price series are reconstructions (splices of several indices); treat year-to-year moves under 1-2% as noise.
- UK wartime (1914-1920) cost of living is the Ministry of Labour index, which was criticised as understating working-class cost rises (fixed 1904 basket). Both ONS and BoE agree 1920 prices were about 2.6x 1913.
- The BoE earnings series is an all-worker average in £/week; manual adult male rates (the Hansard table) run roughly 1.5-2x that average.
- Seamen's pay: the 1925 and 1930 figures are well sourced (NMB rates); 1913 and 1921 fireman figures come from a reputable academic site without a primary citation; 1914-1920 and 1932-1940 are gaps. Pay was monthly plus food (worth roughly 21s/wk per the 1930 Hansard difference between fed and unfed weekly rates).
- Officers' 1912 figures are from the Titanic crew agreement (BT 100/259) as reported by secondary sites; master's salary is not verified.
- Exchange rates are annual averages; within-year swings were large in 1919-1921, 1931-1934 and 1939.
