# How fast shipping lines grew, and what they earned, 1900 to 1939 (0.39.4)

The research behind 0.39.4's balance targets. Test round 5 (`docs/overhaul/test-rounds/round5.md`) turned the starting Line, one old 4,600-ton steamer worth about £25,000, into £1.4m (careful) to £3.5m (bold) by 1914 and £15m to £26m by 1940. Each figure below is marked **S** (sourced, with the link), **R** (recalled by the researcher, not verified) or **I** (inference).

**Targets (inference):** no British owner grew £25,000 into £1.4m by 1914 out of running ships. From the game's start, a careful owner should be near £100,000 to £250,000 by 1914 and a bold, lucky one £0.5m to £1m; by 1939, £0.5m to £3m. More than about £5m should need takeovers on Ellerman's or Kylsant's scale, with Kylsant's risk of collapse.

## Conference fares

Conferences set minimum first and second class fares for each class of ship from 1896, and pooled steerage by quota; a line's premium came from its ships' rating, not free pricing (Cunard's average fares rose 41% in 1907-14, 18% leaving out its express ships) ([NBER w22426](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22426/w22426.pdf); [MPRA 47850](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47850/1/MPRA_paper_47850.pdf)). No source found for an explicit ceiling; the game's ceiling of 15% over the rate stands for the rate set by the class of ship.

## The 1919-20 boom and after

British freight index (1898-1913 = 100): 622 in January 1920, 678 in March, 149 in December 1921 ([FRED, from The Economist](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/M0333BGBM316SNBR)); 9.13m tons idle worldwide on 1 January 1923 ([NBER c4958](https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c4958/c4958.pdf)). Owners who bought at the top were ruined: Western Counties (wound up 1922), Gould (1925), the Town Line (1924). A new 7,500-ton steamer cost about £48,000 in 1913 and £258,000 early in 1920 (R; Paxton gives the same 1920 peak in `earnings.md`).

## Taxes

Standard rate of income tax in the pound: 1s (1900), 1s 2d (1901), 1s 3d (1902), 11d (1903), 1s (1904-08), 1s 2d (1909-14), 3s 6d (1915), 5s (1916-17), 6s (1918-21), 5s (1922), 4s 6d (1923) (mostly R; 1909-14 and 1918-20 S: [UK Parliament](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/private-lives/taxation/overview/firstworldwar/), [Finance Act 1920](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/10-11/18/enacted)). Corporation Profits Tax: 5% of company profits from 1920 to mid-1924 ([Finance Act 1920, s.52](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/10-11/18/enacted); [Finance Act 1924, s.34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/14-15/21/part/III/enacted?view=plain)).

## The Depression

British freight index: 114.6 (1929), 92.3 (1930), 88.3 (1932), 84.6 (1933), 97.1 (1936) ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/M0333BGBM316SNBR)); 23% of British tramps idle in 1933 ([Hansard, 13 December 1933](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1933/dec/13/shipping-industry-and-shipbuilding)).
